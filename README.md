# Consumer Purchase Intention in Online Gaming — India
Second research project, done as a team of 6 at SMIT under the supervision
of Prof. Anosh Lepcha. The question we started with: why do people spend
real money on virtual items that don't exist outside a game? And more
specifically, does the answer look different for Indian gamers compared
to what global research says?

One thing that made this study interesting was that some consumers aren't
just buying for enjoyment. A growing segment purchases in-game items like
skins, weapon cases, virtual goods, with an eye on resale value and
return on investment. Reichenbach (2025) documented Counter-Strike weapon
cases generating average annual returns of 66.9% between 2015–2025,
outperforming stocks and gold. That changes how you think about what's
driving the purchase decision. It's not always irrational or purely
hedonic, sometimes it's calculated.

## How we approached it — three phases
**Phase 1 (completed):** We started broad. Before narrowing into specific
factors, we first mapped the online gaming industry as a whole, how it
evolved, what the Indian market looks like, how in-game purchasing works
at a macro level, and where consumer behaviour research sits within this
space. This was about understanding the full landscape before going deeper.

**Phase 2 (completed):** This is where the real work happened. We did a
systematic review of 13 peer-reviewed studies published between 2009 and
2025, pulling out 12 factors that kept appearing as drivers of in-game
purchase intention across global research:

- SN — Subjective Norm / Social Influence
- SI — Social Interaction
- PE — Perceived Enjoyment
- PV — Perceived Value
- ATT — Attitude
- PR — Price Value
- PU — Perceived Usefulness
- SAT — Satisfaction
- HAB — Habit
- FLOW — Flow Experience
- PEOU — Perceived Ease of Use
- PBC — Perceived Behavioural Control

We ranked these 12 factors by significance rate across all 13 studies.
Perceived Value, Attitude, Satisfaction, and Habit each came out at 100%
significance, the most consistently validated factors in existing
literature.

Then we ran a pilot survey with 18 respondents and analysed it using
Relative Importance Index (RII) in SPSS. We compared how Indian gamers
ranked these 12 factors against how global literature ranked them.

Spearman Rank Correlation: rho = 0.356, p = 0.256.

Weak. Non-significant. Indian gamers ranked Perceived Enjoyment, Habit,
and Satisfaction much higher than global literature does. Factors like
Perceived Value and Social Influence which were dominant in global studies,
ranked lower for Indian respondents.

This misalignment is the central finding of Phase 2. It means a dedicated
India-specific study isn't just useful but rather it's necessary. Global findings
cannot simply be applied to the Indian gaming market.

**Phase 3 (proposed):** Full-scale primary survey targeting 300+ active
Indian online gamers, recruited through Reddit, Discord, and Steam
communities. The method will be PLS-SEM via SmartPLS 4.0, chosen because
the 12 factors are latent constructs that can't be directly measured, and
because PLS-SEM tests all 12 factor-to-purchase-intention relationships
simultaneously in one unified model rather than running separate
regressions. Game genre and spending level will be tested as moderators.

## Methodology snapshot
| Parameter | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Phase 1 | Industry overview, broad consumer behaviour context |
| Phase 2 | Systematic review of 13 studies (2009–2025), 12 factors identified, pilot survey n=18, RII + Spearman Rank Correlation in SPSS |
| Phase 3 (proposed) | Primary survey n=300+, PLS-SEM via SmartPLS 4.0 |
| Key finding | rho = 0.356, p = 0.256 — Indian gamer priorities misalign with global literature |

## What makes this different from prior studies
Most existing research tested 4–8 factors in isolation and focused on
Indonesia, South Korea, and Taiwan. This study reviews all 12 identified
factors together, explicitly includes both rational (PBC, PV) and
irrational/impulsive (HAB, FLOW) purchase drivers, and is the first to
examine this in the Indian context. Phase 3 will use PLS-SEM over
standalone regression, which better handles latent variable relationships
and improves explanatory precision.
