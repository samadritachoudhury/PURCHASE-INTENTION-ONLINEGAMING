# Purchase Intention of Consumers in Online Gaming
Second research project, done as a team of 6 at SMIT under the supervision 
of Prof. Anosh Lepcha. This one started with a question that bothered us for uite someytime.
Why do people spend real money on virtual items that don't exist outside a 
game? Was it just the fact that they were so obsessed with a game that they didnt care about its liability in real world? Well turns out it's lot more complicated than just that.

## What we were trying to figure out
Most research on in-game purchases comes from Indonesia, South Korea, and 
Taiwan. Almost nothing exists for Indian gamers specifically. We wanted to 
know whether the factors that global literature says drive purchase intention 
actually hold for Indian players — or whether Indian gamers are wired 
differently.
Spoiler: they are.

## How we approached it
**Phase 1 (completed):** We started broad. Before diving into specific 
factors, we first mapped the online gaming industry as a whole, how it 
evolved, how big the Indian market is, what in-game purchasing looks like 
at a macro level, and where consumer behaviour research sits within this 
space. This was about understanding the full landscape before narrowing down.

**Phase 2 (completed):** This is where the real analytical work happened. 
We did a systematic review of 13 peer-reviewed studies published between 
2009 and 2025, pulling out 12 factors that kept appearing as drivers of 
in-game purchase intention across global research:

- SN (Subjective Norm / Social Influence)
- SI (Social Interaction)
- PE (Perceived Enjoyment)
- PV (Perceived Value)
- ATT (Attitude)
- PR (Price Value)
- PU (Perceived Usefulness)
- SAT (Satisfaction)
- HAB (Habit)
- FLOW (Flow Experience)
- PEOU (Perceived Ease of Use)
- PBC (Perceived Behavioural Control)

We ranked these by significance rate across all 13 studies. Perceived Value, 
Attitude, Satisfaction, and Habit each came out at 100% significance,
the most consistently validated factors in existing literature.

Then we ran a pilot survey with 18 respondents and analysed it using RII 
(Relative Importance Index) in SPSS. We compared how Indian gamers ranked 
these 12 factors against how global literature ranked them.

The Spearman Rank Correlation came out at rho = 0.356, p = 0.256.

Weak. Non-significant. Meaning Indian gamers don't prioritise the same 
factors that dominate global research. Perceived Enjoyment, Habit, and 
Satisfaction ranked much higher for Indian respondents than factors like 
Perceived Value and Social Influence — which global studies consistently 
flag as dominant.

This misalignment is the central finding of Phase 2. It tells us a 
dedicated India-specific study isn't just useful — it's necessary.

**Phase 3 (proposed):** Full-scale primary survey targeting 300+ active 
Indian online gamers, recruited through Reddit, Discord, and Steam gaming 
communities. The method will be PLS-SEM (SmartPLS 4.0) — chosen because 
the 12 factors are latent constructs that can't be directly measured, and 
because PLS-SEM tests all 12 factor-to-purchase-intention relationships 
simultaneously in one unified model. Game genre and spending level will 
also be tested as moderators.

## Methodology snapshot
| Parameter | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| Phase 1 | Industry overview, broad consumer behaviour context |
| Phase 2 | Systematic review of 13 studies (2009–2025), 12 factors identified, pilot survey n=18, RII + Spearman Rank Correlation in SPSS |
| Phase 3 (proposed) | Primary survey n=300+, PLS-SEM via SmartPLS 4.0 |
| Key finding | rho = 0.356, p = 0.256 — Indian gamer priorities misalign with global literature |
